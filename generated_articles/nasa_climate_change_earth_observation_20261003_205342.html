NASA Monitors Rapid Intensification of Hurricane Polo

NASA utilized its Earth observation capabilities to track Hurricane Polo, a powerful Category 5 storm in the Eastern Pacific, which underwent exceptionally rapid intensification in September 2026.

01

Observing Hurricane Polo

On September 25, 2026, the Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) instrument aboard the Suomi National Polar orbiting Partnership (Suomi NPP) platform captured a Black Marble nighttime blue/yellow composite image of Hurricane Polo. This imagery revealed a prominent and deep eye, indicative of the storm's intensity. NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS) and Worldview provide access to near real-time and historical imagery, including observations of such events.

02

Analysis of Rapid Intensification

Hurricane Polo intensified from a tropical storm to a Category 5 hurricane in just 24 hours, with wind speeds increasing by 90 knots (170 kilometers per hour or 105 miles per hour). Gary Partyka, an atmospheric scientist with the Global Modeling and Assimilation Office (GMAO) at NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center, described this as 'explosively rapid intensification.' The storm developed in an environment characterized by weak wind shear, high moisture, warmer ocean temperatures, and high levels of atmospheric instability, which Partyka noted were 'near perfect' conditions for strengthening.

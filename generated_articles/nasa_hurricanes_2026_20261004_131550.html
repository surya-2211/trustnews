NASA Monitors Intense 2026 Hurricane Season in the Pacific

NASA instruments and scientists closely monitored a highly active 2026 hurricane season in the Pacific Ocean, characterized by multiple powerful storms, including several reaching Category 5 strength. The season saw rapid intensification events and simultaneous hurricane occurrences, with El Niño conditions contributing to increased storm development in the Eastern Pacific.

SECTION 01

Eastern Pacific Sees Multiple Major Hurricanes

The Eastern Pacific experienced significant hurricane activity in 2026. On September 25, 2026, Category 5 Hurricane Polo was observed moving slowly westward over warm waters. Forecasters predicted it would make landfall on Baja California Sur around September 28. Polo underwent what an atmospheric scientist with NASA’s Global Modeling and Assimilation Office (GMAO) described as "explosively rapid intensification," transforming from a tropical storm to a Category 5 hurricane in just 24 hours, with wind speeds increasing by 90 knots (170 kilometers per hour or 105 miles per hour). It maintained maximum sustained winds of 180 mph (285 km/h) and fluctuated between Category 4 and 5 strength for several days. These conditions were attributed to weak wind shear, high moisture, warmer ocean temperatures, and high levels of atmospheric instability. To the west of Polo, Hurricane Odalys, a Category 4 storm with maximum sustained winds near 130 mph (215 km/h), was also present but was not expected to approach land. Earlier in the season, in late August and early September 2026, Hurricanes Lowell, Karina, and Marie formed in the Eastern Pacific. Warming in the Eastern Pacific Ocean caused by El Niño created favorable conditions for these storms. Lowell and Karina were predicted to reach Category 3 or 4 strength, while Marie was expected to approach Category 3. The simultaneous presence of three hurricanes in the Eastern or Central Pacific was last observed in 2015, also during an El Niño year.

SECTION 02

Super Typhoon Dolphin Reaches Category 5 in Northwest Pacific

In the Northwest Pacific Ocean, Super Typhoon Dolphin reached Category 5 strength on July 30, 2026, with maximum sustained winds of 165 mph (270 km/hr). This marked the fifth Category 5 cyclone of 2026. Dolphin intensified rapidly from a tropical storm to a super typhoon in just two days, driven by warm ocean waters between 84°F and 86°F (29°C to 30°C) and low wind shear. While not posing an immediate threat to land, the storm was predicted to approach the Ogasawara Islands as a strong typhoon by August 3, followed by the Nansei Islands (Ryukyu Islands) and the East China Sea.

SECTION 03

NASA's Observational Role

NASA played a crucial role in monitoring these intense weather events. The Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) instrument, aboard both the Suomi National Polar-orbiting Partnership (Suomi NPP) and NOAA-21 platforms, acquired images of the storms. For instance, a Black Marble nighttime blue/yellow composite of Hurricane Polo was acquired by VIIRS on Suomi NPP on September 25, 2026, highlighting its prominent and deep eye. Similarly, VIIRS on the NOAA-21 platform captured images of Hurricanes Lowell, Karina, and Marie on September 2, 2026, and a true-color corrected reflectance image of Super Typhoon Dolphin on July 31, 2026. NASA’s Earth Science Data Systems (ESDS) Program, through its Earthdata and Worldview platforms, provided access to near real-time and historical imagery, enabling detailed visualization and analysis of these powerful hurricanes. Data from the Group for High Resolution Sea Surface Temperature (GHRSST) project also contributed to understanding the warm ocean waters fueling these storms.

What matters

  • Hurricane Polo reached Category 5 strength in the Eastern Pacific on September 25, 2026, undergoing "explosively rapid intensification."
  • Three hurricanes—Lowell, Karina, and Marie—were simultaneously active in the Eastern Pacific in early September 2026, a phenomenon linked to El Niño conditions.
  • Super Typhoon Dolphin became the fifth Category 5 cyclone of 2026 in the Northwest Pacific on July 30, 2026.
  • NASA instruments, particularly the VIIRS on Suomi NPP and NOAA-21, were used to monitor these intense storms.

The takeaway

The 2026 hurricane season in the Pacific demonstrated significant activity and intensity, with multiple Category 5 storms and simultaneous hurricane formations. NASA's advanced satellite instruments and scientific analysis played a vital role in observing and understanding these powerful weather events, providing critical data on their development and potential impacts.

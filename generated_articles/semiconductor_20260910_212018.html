Innovations in Semiconductor Technology: From Living Transistors to Flexible Chips

Semiconductor

Recent advancements in materials science and bioengineering are pushing the boundaries of semiconductor technology, leading to novel approaches in computing and electronic device fabrication. Researchers at MIT and other institutions are exploring new ways to create transistors and develop next-generation chips and flexible electronics.

Living Transistors for Biological Computing

According to MIT News, engineers have successfully connected bacteria to create 'living transistors.' These bacterial transistors can be wired together into circuits capable of performing complex calculations. ScienceDaily further elaborated that these living circuits can also direct chemical signals. Potential future applications for these biological computers include coating plant roots or leaves to detect environmental threats and automatically trigger defenses, according to ScienceDaily.

Flexible and Transparent Next-Generation Chips

MIT News highlights a new fabrication platform that could enable flexible, transparent next-generation photonic chips. This scalable process is capable of producing high-performance chips suitable for applications such as discreet wearables or pliable augmented-reality displays, as stated by MIT News. Additionally, ScienceDaily reported a breakthrough in chemistry, allowing scientists to create nanocrystals from tough metal nitrides. These 'impossible' nanocrystals, previously difficult to produce at scale, could transform familiar materials used in LEDs, implants, and superconductors into building blocks for flexible electronics, printable devices, and other technologies, ScienceDaily noted.

AI-Assisted Material Design

In a related development, MIT News reported on an AI tool named 'CrysVCD' that assists in designing new materials. This tool could significantly reduce the time and cost associated with screening out chemically unstable designs, helping to accelerate the development of materials that work in real-world applications.
